    Glossary · Mulliken symbols

    What do A, B, g and u mean?

    ABgu1 2 3 · Σ ΠDecoder

    Every orbital, vibration and electronic state carries a Mulliken symbol, such as Ag or B3u. Each part of the symbol records how the object responds to one particular symmetry operation: +1 if it is unchanged, −1 if it changes sign. Use Show in 3D to watch each test on an O2 orbital.

    ASymmetric under rotation

    A one-dimensional representation whose character under rotation about the principal axis is +1: the orbital is unchanged by the turn.

    TestApply the principal Cn. Same orbital, same colours → A.

    D2h has three perpendicular C2 axes and none outranks the others, so the rule is stated for all three: A means χ = +1 under every C2.

    In O2The σg orbitals (2σg and 3σg) are Ag: whichever axis you turn about, every lobe lands on a lobe of the same colour.

    BAntisymmetric under rotation

    A one-dimensional representation whose character under rotation about the principal axis is −1: the turn puts every lobe on a lobe of the opposite colour.

    TestApply the principal Cn. Same shape, colours swapped → B.

    In D2h, a B representation is −1 under two of the three C2 rotations and +1 under the third. The number tells you which one gives +1:

    • B1: symmetric under C2(z), the O–O axis
    • B2: symmetric under C2(y)
    • B3: symmetric under C2(x)

    In O2The bonding π orbital along x, 1πu(x), is B3u. A 180° turn about the O–O axis swaps its top and bottom lobes, so χ(C2(z)) = −1.

    gGerade: symmetric under inversion

    From the German gerade, "even". Inversion i sends every point (x, y, z) through the centre of the molecule to (−x, −y, −z). A g orbital has χ(i) = +1: every lobe lands on a lobe of the same colour.

    TestApply i. Colours unchanged → g.

    In O22σg, 3σg (σ bonding) and 1πg* (π antibonding).

    uUngerade: antisymmetric under inversion

    From ungerade, "odd". A u orbital has χ(i) = −1: inversion puts every lobe on a lobe of the opposite colour.

    TestApply i. Colours swapped → u.

    In O22σu*, 3σu* (σ antibonding) and 1πu (π bonding).

    PatternFor a homonuclear diatomic, σ bonding is g and σ* is u, but π bonding is u and π* is g. Only molecules with an inversion centre get g/u labels: O2 does, CO does not.

    …The rest of the symbol

    • Subscripts 1, 2, 3: in D2h, which C2 axis (z, y, x) the representation is symmetric under. In groups with one principal axis, 1/2 means symmetric/antisymmetric under a perpendicular C2 or, if there is none, a σv.
    • E and T: two- and three-dimensional (degenerate) representations. They never apply to D2h.
    • Σ, Π, Δ: linear molecules (D∞h, C∞v) use Greek letters. Σ is one-dimensional, like A. Π and Δ are doubly degenerate. In orbital names they become σ, π, δ: zero, one or two nodal planes containing the bond axis.
    • Superscript + / − on Σ: symmetric / antisymmetric under the mirror planes σv that contain the axis.
    • ′ / ″: symmetric / antisymmetric under a horizontal mirror plane σh (not needed for O2).
    • Lower case (ag, b3u, σg, πu) is used for single orbitals; upper case for representations and electronic states, such as the ground state of O2, 3Σg−.
    • Asterisk *: not a symmetry label, just a reminder that an orbital is antibonding (1πg*).
